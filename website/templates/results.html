Results & Key Insights

Major findings from the analysis — what drives house prices, which features matter most, and what the data reveals about the Ames housing market.

Key Findings

#1

Overall Quality Dominates

OverallQual has the highest correlation with SalePrice (0.79). A one-point increase in quality (on a 1-10 scale) corresponds to roughly a $25,000–40,000 increase in sale price. This single feature explains more variance than any other.

#2

Living Area is King

GrLivArea (above-ground living area) is the second strongest predictor (correlation: 0.71). Each additional 100 sq ft of living space adds approximately $8,000–12,000 to the price. The relationship is remarkably linear, which explains why Linear Regression performs so well.

#3

Renovation Impact

Recently remodeled houses command a significant premium. The RemodAge feature (years since last remodel) has a negative correlation with price — meaning more recent renovations lead to higher prices. A house remodeled within the last 5 years sells for 15–25% more than a similar unrenovated property.

#4

Garage Matters More Than Pool

Having a garage (present in 95% of homes) correlates strongly with price, while pools (only 0.5% of homes) have minimal impact. In the Midwest, covered parking is a necessity while pools are a luxury with limited seasonal use. GarageCars and GarageArea are among the top 10 predictors.

#5

Neighborhood Premium

Location matters enormously. The top 3 neighborhoods (NridgHt, NoRidge, StoneBr) have median prices 2–3× higher than budget neighborhoods (MeadowV, BrDale). This reflects school districts, amenities, and overall desirability — factors captured purely through location.

#6

Linear Regression Wins

Despite being the simplest model, Linear Regression achieves the highest R² score. This is because our feature engineering explicitly creates the non-linear relationships (interactions, binary flags) that linear models can't discover on their own. The log transform of the target also linearizes the problem.

Visual Analysis

Feature Importance (Random Forest)

This chart shows which features the Random Forest model relies on most when making predictions. The importance score indicates how much each feature reduces prediction error when used for splitting decisions in the trees.

Interpretation

Engineered features like TotalSF and OverallScore appear prominently, validating our feature engineering decisions. The model confirms that total square footage and overall quality/condition are the primary value drivers. Interestingly, HouseAge ranks higher than the raw YearBuilt, showing that the engineered feature provides a cleaner signal.

Top 10 Neighborhoods by Median Price

Neighborhood is the strongest categorical predictor. The premium neighborhoods (Northridge Heights, Northridge, Stone Brook) achieve median prices above $300,000, while the overall dataset median is around $163,000.

Interpretation

Location acts as a proxy for many unmeasured factors: school quality, crime rates, proximity to amenities, and community desirability. The one-hot encoding of Neighborhood creates binary features that let the model learn a distinct price premium or discount for each location.

Price Trend by Year Built

Newer homes consistently sell for more, with a strong upward trend from the 1950s onward. Homes built after 2000 command the highest prices, reflecting modern construction standards, energy efficiency, and updated designs.

Interpretation

The trend isn't strictly linear — there are plateaus and dips corresponding to different construction eras. This non-linearity is why our HouseAge feature (which linearizes the relationship) outperforms raw YearBuilt in most models.

Mean Absolute Error

MAE shows the average dollar amount by which predictions differ from actual prices. Lower is better. The best model's predictions are off by approximately this amount on average.

Root Mean Squared Error

RMSE penalizes large errors more heavily than MAE. If RMSE is much larger than MAE, it indicates the model occasionally makes very large errors (outlier predictions).
